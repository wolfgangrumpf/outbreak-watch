# Research playbook

## Per sweep
- 10–20 searches, and fetch the key articles; don't rely on snippets for numbers.
- At minimum, search in **the local language and English**. Add the languages of neighboring countries when border measures are in play (for Irkutsk: Russian, English, Mongolian, Kazakh, Chinese).
- Search for "what's new since lastSweep": date-qualified queries, outlet-specific queries, and the names of officials.

## Source tiers (adapt per country)
1. **Official:** national health agency (e.g. Rospotrebnadzor), regional government, WHO (DON, spokesperson), ECDC, CDC, and neighboring health ministries (e.g. Mongolia NCZD).
2. **Independent local:** for Russia, that meant Lyudi Baikala, Meduza, Moscow Times, The Insider, Current Time/Sibir.Realii, RFE/RL and Radio Svoboda.
3. **State media:** TASS, RIA, RG. Record what the state line is.
4. **Regional or city sites** near the nexus, and in transport-hub cities.
5. **International wires and press:** Reuters, AP, AFP, CNN, NBC, CNBC, Axios, Newsweek, Euronews, Forbes.
6. **Aggregators** (e.g. plaguenews.com, outbreakwatch.fyi) are for leads only. Cite the underlying source, not the aggregator homepage.
7. **Telegram or social media claims** are always `unverified` unless corroborated.

## Labeling
- Many outlets relaying one origin is still **reported**, not confirmed.
- An official denial of an independent report makes the item **disputed**. Keep both in the note.
- Pro-government "fact-check" sites are evidence of the official line, not independent confirmation.
- A foreign ministry paraphrasing the origin country's findings is a `reported` claim about that wording.

## Escalation (raise only on evidence)
Notify on any of these: the level rose, a confirmed secondary case, a case outside the nexus region, or a case at a transport hub. Put it in the first line of the final message.

## Assessment habits
- Count days since the index date against incubation; name the date when the cohort clears.
- Separate epidemiological risk from information-quality risk.
- Name the nearest population centers and hubs, with distances.
